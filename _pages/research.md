## Pharmacoepidemiology and Real-World Evidence

Randomized controlled trials provide essential evidence on the efficacy and safety of medical treatments. However, important questions remain regarding their effectiveness, safety, and optimal use in routine clinical practice, particularly among diverse patient populations and over extended follow-up periods. Real-world healthcare data offer valuable opportunities to address these evidence gaps and inform clinical and therapeutic decision-making.

My research focuses on evaluating medication effectiveness, safety, utilization, and treatment strategies using electronic health records, administrative claims, and other observational data sources. I conduct real-world studies across the full research lifecycle, including research question development, study design, protocol and statistical analysis plan development, data collection and management, statistical analysis, quality assurance, and scientific reporting. My primary areas of interest include:

- **Medication effectiveness and safety:** Evaluating the benefits and risks of medical treatments in real-world patient populations.
- **Treatment initiation and selection:** Investigating the effects of initiating treatments and selecting among alternative therapeutic options.
- **Treatment timing and strategies:** Evaluating treatment timing, sequencing, combination therapy, and sustained treatment strategies.
- **Cardiometabolic and related diseases:** Studying therapeutic interventions for diabetes, cardiovascular disease, chronic kidney disease, and liver disease.

## Causal Inference and Methodological Research

Observational healthcare data offer important opportunities to investigate clinical questions that may be difficult or impractical to address through randomized trials. However, estimating causal effects from observational data presents methodological challenges, including confounding, selection bias, time-varying exposures, informative outcome ascertainment, and missing data.

My methodological research focuses on developing, evaluating, and applying causal inference methods to strengthen the validity and interpretability of evidence generated from observational studies. I integrate modern causal inference frameworks with longitudinal statistical modeling and simulation-based investigations to address complex methodological challenges. My areas of interest include:

- **Target trial emulation:** Designing observational studies that emulate hypothetical randomized trials to estimate the causal effects of treatment strategies.
- **Longitudinal causal inference:** Applying inverse probability weighting, the parametric g-formula, and standardization to address time-varying confounding and estimate causal effects.
- **Complex treatment strategies:** Using methods such as clone-censor-weighting to evaluate treatment initiation, timing, and sustained treatment strategies.
- **Bias and methodological evaluation:** Investigating informative outcome ascertainment, missing data, model specification, and other sources of bias through applied research and simulation studies.

## Health Economics and Outcomes Research and Health Technology Assessment

Healthcare decisions require evidence not only on the clinical effectiveness and safety of medical interventions but also on their economic consequences, resource utilization, and overall value. Health economics and outcomes research (HEOR) and health technology assessment (HTA) provide complementary approaches to evaluating healthcare interventions and informing resource allocation, reimbursement, and policy decisions.

My research in this area integrates clinical and economic evidence to evaluate the outcomes and value of healthcare interventions. By combining real-world evidence, economic evaluation, and evidence synthesis, I aim to support evidence-based decision-making across healthcare systems and the life sciences. My areas of interest include:

- **Healthcare resource utilization and costs:** Evaluating healthcare utilization, treatment-related costs, and economic outcomes associated with medical interventions.
- **Economic evaluation:** Applying cost-effectiveness analysis, cost-utility analysis, and decision-analytic modeling to assess the economic value of healthcare interventions.
- **Evidence synthesis:** Integrating clinical and economic evidence through systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and quantitative evidence synthesis.
- **Health technology and value assessment:** Generating and synthesizing evidence to inform health technology assessment, reimbursement, market access, healthcare policy, and strategic decision-making.
