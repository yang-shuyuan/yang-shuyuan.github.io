## Medication Effectiveness, Safety, and Treatment Strategies in Routine Clinical Practice

Randomized clinical trials provide essential evidence on the efficacy and safety of medical treatments. Real-world evidence (RWE) complements clinical trial findings by addressing questions regarding medication utilization, effectiveness, and safety in routine clinical practice, particularly across diverse patient populations and over extended follow-up periods. Such evidence can inform clinical practice, post-marketing surveillance, regulatory evaluation, and decision-making throughout the pharmaceutical product lifecycle. My research focuses on evaluating medication use and treatment strategies using electronic health records, administrative claims, and other observational data sources. My work spans the full research lifecycle, including study design, protocol and statistical analysis plan development, data collection and management, statistical analysis, quality assurance, and scientific reporting. These include:

- **Medication utilization, effectiveness, and safety:** Investigating patterns of medication use and evaluating treatment benefits and risks in real-world populations.
- **Treatment strategies:** Evaluating treatment initiation, selection, timing, sequencing, combination therapy, and sustained treatment strategies.
- **Therapeutic applications:** Investigating pharmacological interventions for cardiometabolic, kidney, and liver diseases.

## Causal Inference and Bias in Longitudinal Observational Studies

Observational healthcare data provide opportunities to address important clinical and public health questions that may be difficult or impractical to investigate through randomized trials. However, valid causal inference from observational data requires careful consideration of methodological challenges, including confounding, selection bias, time-varying exposures, informative outcome ascertainment, and missing data. My methodological research focuses on the application and evaluation of causal inference methods to improve the validity and interpretability of observational evidence. I integrate causal inference frameworks, longitudinal statistical methods, and simulation studies to address methodological challenges in the design and analysis of observational research. These include:

- **Target trial emulation:** Designing observational studies that explicitly emulate hypothetical randomized trials to estimate the causal effects of treatment strategies.
- **Longitudinal causal inference:** Applying inverse probability weighting, the parametric g-formula, standardization, and clone-censor-weighting to estimate causal effects under complex longitudinal treatment strategies.
- **Bias and methodological evaluation:** Investigating potential sources of bias, including confounding, selection bias, informative outcome ascertainment, and missing data, through methodological investigations and simulation studies.

## Economic Evaluation and Value Assessment of Healthcare Interventions

Evaluating healthcare interventions requires consideration of their clinical effectiveness, safety, economic consequences, and broader implications for healthcare systems. Health economics and outcomes research (HEOR) and health technology assessment (HTA) provide complementary approaches to integrating clinical, economic, and patient-centered evidence to assess the value of healthcare interventions. My research focuses on the integration of real-world evidence, economic evaluation, decision-analytic modeling, and evidence synthesis to inform healthcare resource allocation, reimbursement, market access, and health policy decisions. These include:

- **Healthcare resource utilization and costs:** Assessing healthcare utilization, treatment-related costs, and economic outcomes associated with medical interventions.
- **Economic evaluation:** Applying cost-effectiveness analysis, cost-utility analysis, and decision-analytic modeling to evaluate the economic value of healthcare interventions.
- **Evidence synthesis and value assessment:** Integrating clinical and economic evidence through systematic reviews, meta-analyses, and health technology assessment to support evidence-based healthcare decision-making.
