I am a Postdoctoral Research Associate in the Department of Epidemiology at Brown University. My research focuses on applying modern causal inference methods to real-world healthcare data to evaluate the effectiveness and safety of medical treatments.

My work spans real-world evidence, pharmacoepidemiology, and comparative effectiveness research, with a particular interest in cardiometabolic diseases, chronic kidney disease, and liver disease.

## Research Interests

- Real-World Evidence and Pharmacoepidemiology
- Causal Inference and Target Trial Emulation
- Comparative Effectiveness and Medication Safety
- Cardiometabolic, Kidney, and Liver Diseases

## Methodological Expertise

My methodological experience includes target trial emulation, propensity score methods, inverse probability weighting, clone-censor-weighting, and longitudinal and time-to-event analysis.

I work with electronic health records and other longitudinal observational data using SAS and R.
